0:45What You're Looking At

Establish the two paths without diving into ports and topics yet.

say"Both windows are playing the exact same video, from the exact same source machine, over the exact same degraded link right now — think a tactical radio link, or any connection that's lossy and jittery, not fully down."
say"Left is the video sent the ordinary way: straight UDP, fire and forget, no broker involved. Right is the same video, published into a Solace PubSub+ broker, bridged across that same degraded link to a second broker, and served back out as RTSP."

3:15Why One Froze

The mechanism, in one breath, not a protocol lecture.

say"UDP has no memory. Once a packet's gone, it's gone — the decoder hits a hole it can't fill and just gives up. That's the freeze."
say"Solace's connection to the broker runs over plain TCP underneath. TCP already knows how to notice a gap and ask for it again — transparently, before the video layer ever sees a problem. Nothing custom-built for video; it's just what TCP always does."
doOptional, if there's a Manager UI tab open: point at the bridge queue's message count still climbing steadily. Don't dwell — keep this segment under two minutes.
5:15The Trust Reveal

This is the moment that turns skepticism into buy-in — earn it by doing it live, not by claiming it.

say"Just so you know we're not fooling you — here's the same link, with the degradation switched off."
doSwitch to the shaper terminal. Send the prepared tc qdisc del dev $IFACE root. Switch back to the screen share.
say"Watch the left side recover."
doLet it play clean for a few seconds. Both windows now look equally good — that's the point, not a letdown.
If asked why bother, then "Because on a clean link, plain UDP is actually the better choice — lighter, lower latency, nothing extra in the way. Solace's overhead only earns its keep once the link stops being clean. That's the trade you're making, and it's a deliberate one."

8:00Q&A — Prepared Ground

Five questions this room is likely to ask, answered from what was actually measured, not the brochure version.

QIs Solace just running its own version of UDP under the hood?
No — the client's connection to the broker, and the bridge between brokers, both run over plain TCP. The resilience you saw isn't a Solace-specific trick; it's TCP doing what TCP always does, with Solace's guaranteed-message handling layered on top for the parts that need to survive a full outage, not just jitter.
QWhat if the link doesn't just degrade — it goes fully dark?
Different mechanism, same underlying answer: messages published as guaranteed get spooled in a durable queue and redelivered once the link's back, so nothing published during the outage is lost. A live video subscriber just resumes in real time once reconnected rather than replaying the gap — the queue is what gives you the option to replay, if that's what the use case needs.
QDoesn't all this cost you something when the link is fine?
Yes, honestly — you just saw it. Direct UDP has less overhead and no broker hop, so on a clean link it's the leaner choice. The trade only pays off once the link degrades, which is exactly the scenario this demo is built to show.
QCould the queue itself fill up and start dropping messages?
Yes — it has a spool quota, sized deliberately rather than left at a default. That's a real, tunable ceiling on how long a catch-up buffer can absorb an outage before something has to give, and it's worth sizing to the outage durations you actually expect.
QIs this demo staged — is the frozen video a canned clip?
That's exactly what the trust reveal a few minutes ago was for. The shaping was removed live, on the same running link, and the frozen side recovered in real time. Happy to do it again.
